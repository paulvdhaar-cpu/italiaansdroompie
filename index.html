Our Story

To a hilltop
in Le Marche

Lisette and David came here on holiday and ended up staying. They had three young kids, a feeling that life back home was running a little too fast, and one afternoon up in Monte Vidon Combatte where the valley opened up below them and they looked at each other and knew. The campsite has been welcoming guests since 2007, Lisette and David have run it since 2026, and between them they've made it something that feels entirely their own.

They've kept the campsite small on purpose. Eighteen pitches, a handful of glamping tents, a pool, a terrace, an olive grove. No animation team, no entertainment programme, no resort feeling. Just a family-run place where Lisette and David are usually around, and will probably end up pouring you a glass of wine before you've made it back to your tent.

A proper campsite, not a resort

Kids run around barefoot. You can actually relax. Mornings start with fresh bread on the terrace and evenings tend to last longer than you planned.

Life on the Hill

Things to do —
as little or as much

The honest answer is: not much, and that's the point. But if you want to do something, there's plenty. A hilltop, a swimming pool, twenty minutes to the Adriatic, and a week without screens that will surprise you.

All summer
The pool

Kids in the water, parents with a book on the edge, the valley below hazy in the afternoon heat.

Twice a week
The long table

Lisette and David cook. Local food, Centanni wine, neighbours and guests mixed together. You end up staying until midnight and you're fine with that.

20 minutes away
Grottammare

Sandy, calm, more Italians than tourists. Gelato on the way back is part of the deal.

40 minutes inland
The Sibillini

Peaks at nearly 2500m, wildflower meadows in summer, snow on the tops well into spring. Visible from the campsite on a clear day.

Worth the drive
The hill towns

Fermo, Ascoli Piceno, Loreto. Marble squares, no tour buses, long lunches. Lisette and David will tell you which ones are worth it.

On site
For the kids

Playhouse, slide, swings, a big trampoline, table tennis. And a whole hillside to disappear into.

The Region

Le Marche —
the part of Italy most people still miss

Le Marche sits between the Apennines and the Adriatic. Most tourists drive past it on their way to Tuscany or Puglia, which is exactly why it still feels the way Italy used to feel. From the campsite you can be in the mountains in under an hour or at the sea in twenty minutes.

On Site

Simple things,
done well

Terrace & Bar

Fresh bread and cappuccino every morning. Twice a week in high season, an Italian dinner on the long table. No reservations needed.

Swimming Pool
Five by ten metres, open nine to eight, looking out over the valley. Where the kids spend most of their day and where you'll spend most of yours.
Kids Playground
Playhouse, slide, swings, a big trampoline, table tennis. And a whole hillside to disappear into.
Organic Wine
From Giacomo Centanni, three valleys over. Organic, and always on site.
Free Wi-Fi
On the terrace and in the kids' area. Elsewhere we trust you'll cope.
Olive & Herb Gardens
Olive trees, lavender, oleander. Guests can join the harvest in September.
Local Advice
Lisette & David's own list of markets, restaurants, hidden beaches and the spots the guidebooks haven't found.
